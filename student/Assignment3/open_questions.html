Question 1: Training Dynamics

We see that the LSTM has mild overfitting, as the F1 training continued to improve, the validation F1 seemed to plateaued a little after epoh 15 and even slightly declined. We see in the loss curve that there is extreme divergence in the sense that the training and validation loss did not continue learning, we see the training/the model most likely memorizing the training patterns as the loss of the validation did not continue decreasing. When looking at the MLP, we also see signs of overfitting in the sense that the training loss continues declining across the epohs while the validation loss leveled out. There are a number of things you could do to address this such as regularization techniques like weight decay or early stopping or reducing the number of epochs. Perhaps even changing the model capactiy by adding more hidden units could help too. Class weights affects training stability and final performance by affecting the class imbalance by addressing it b assigning higher penalities to misclassiciations of the minority class. This improved macro F1 scores as the model encourages learning of representation for all the categories rather than just the majority class. However, it seems that there is some instability in training most likely due to this.

Question 2: Model Performance and Error Analysis

Between the models, MLP generalized slightly better to the test set. Although both models showed signs of overfitting, LSTM showed a much larger gap between training and validation performance. The LSTM's training accuracy exceeded 0.9 while validation accruacy plateaued at around 0.75. IN contrast the MLP showed a smaller gap between training and validation metric. Also the LSTM's validation loss increased more sharply in later epochs. Also the MLP showed more confusion between Neutral and Positive classes, with 82 samples miscalssified as positive that were neutral samples.

The most frequently misclassified classes were Neutral and positive. For LSTM, 73 positive samples were predicted as neutral while 44 neutral samples were predicted as positive. for the mlp the confusion was even more pronounced with 82 neutral samples misclassified as positive. Reasons for this may be that financial language could contain subtle sentiment cues where posiive statements that are mild can resemble neutral case. The overlap between these categories makes them more difficult to tell compared to negative statements.

Question 3: Cross-Model Comparison

Out of all the models, BERT had the highest accruacy at about 80.5 percent, GRU at 76.8 percent, and LSTM at 75 percent, GPT at around 74 percent,and MLP and RNN at the lowest about 71 percent. The MLP relied on mean pooled FastText embeddings which loses sequential and context information which makes it harder to capture the compoisitional meaning. However, sequence based models process the tokens in order which allow them to model the relationships and dependencies between words. This shows why MLP's lower performance result happened compared to the transformer based models.

The LSTM sequential processing helps capture long range dependencies through gating mechanisms. It also allows it to retain contextual information across the sentence, unlike the MLP which treats the input as an average of embeddings. The gated structure helps deal with vanishing gradients and learn better the patterns and sentiment cues.

The fine-tuned LLMS outperformered the baseline models most likely due to t the large pretraining on text. Pretraining helps the models learn already rich representations before fine tuning. Unlike FastText static embeddings, transformer models generate context dependent word representations. This means that the same word can have multiple different embeddings depending on the surrounding words. This substantially improves sentiment classifiation performance.

The ranking for RNN was worst most likely due to the challenge of modeling long range dependences. MLP slightly did better than RNN but lacked the sequential modeling. LSTM and GRU improved performance by modeling the sequential dependences and also solving the problem fo vanishing gradients. GPT also benefited from transformer based pretraining but could be llimited by the autoregressive nature of the architecture. BERT did the best due to most likely the bidirectional context representations and large scale pretraining, capturing more nuanced patterns.
